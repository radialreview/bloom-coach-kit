# Demo script — paste-ready answers for Mike's fictional practice

For the projector machine. Coaches answer with their *real* practices; this is the stand-in
practice for a facilitator who isn't a coach. Everything below uses one consistent fictional
roster, so every demo moment reinforces the last — and it's built around **Riverbend
Logistics**, which your live April already has on file.

Two things to say out loud when using it: the companies are made up, and your calendar is your
real one — so when the scheduler reads it, the room sees real Bloom-day-job events, which is
its own honest demo of "it actually looked."

**The folder:** `Documents\clients\` holds the matching fake client files — overviews, session
notes, Quarterly Priorities, a Quarterly-prep scratchpad — four companies, nine files, all
consistent with this script's canon. That's what makes the prep demo land: *"Get me ready for
Thursday's Bloom Weekly with Riverbend"* comes back sourced from notes the room can see exist.
Mention it once: "this is my sample client folder — yours is your real one."

---

## The interview (six questions)

**1. Name** — let it suggest, then pick, or type one. If demoing fresh on a clean machine:
> Sol

**2. Personality** — tap:
> Playful

**3. How should they address you?**
> Mike

**4. Tell me about your practice** — THE paste. This is the one you tell the room to spend
their words on, so your own answer has to model it:

> I run a solo coaching practice — eight client companies on Bloom Growth OS. Three came on
> this year and are still finding their feet with the Weekly rhythm: Riverbend Logistics,
> Hartwell Dental, and Copper Peak Builders. The other five are two-plus years in — Lakeshore
> Staffing, Bright Harbor Insurance, Summit Veterinary Group, Ironwood Manufacturing, and
> Prairie Sky Media. Riverbend meets Thursdays; Dana Whitfield is their Integrator and my main
> contact.
>
> A typical week: four or five Bloom Weeklies, mostly mornings. Quarterlies cluster at the
> quarter boundaries — I have three landing in September. I keep Tuesday and Friday afternoons
> light for prep and pipeline. Ironwood is my one drive; everyone else is video.
>
> The part I'd most like off my plate is the before-and-after of sessions. Prep — walking in
> already knowing where each team left off, which To-Dos went quiet, and who's carrying a
> Quarterly Priority that's slipping — and then the follow-up emails afterward. And I
> chronically under-invest in my own pipeline: I know I should make outreach calls every day,
> and client work eats those hours first.

**5. Which specialists?** — keep all four selected.

**6. The one recurring thing you'd hand them tomorrow:**
> Session prep. I want to walk into every client session already knowing where we left off,
> without the hour of digging.

## Grow the team (slides 10–11)

The hire, as on the slide:

> After my sessions I scribble notes and never do anything with them — I want a specialist who
> turns my brain-dump into a recap.

When it asks what should come back:

> A clean recap: what happened, what was decided, who owes what by when, and anything I should
> be worried about.

Then — new session — **the brain-dump**. This is the payoff paste; it's deliberately messy,
real, and full of Bloom vocabulary:

> Here's my brain-dump from today's Riverbend Weekly. Energy was low — Dana ran the meeting
> mostly solo because Marcus was out again, second week running. They finally closed the
> warehouse-lease To-Do. Quarterly Priorities: new-driver onboarding is done, the route-density
> push is on track, but the CRM migration hasn't moved in three weeks and nobody in the room
> would own it. KPI board: on-time delivery dipped under 94 again. The O&O list is getting
> long — they've parked the dispatcher-turnover conversation three weeks straight. I committed
> to sending Dana the org chart worksheet before Thursday. And Dana pulled me aside after: she
> thinks Marcus may be checked out. Keep that one close.

What to point at when the recap comes back: the owed items with owners and dates, the pattern
it names (three weeks of parking the same conversation), and — if you're comfortable narrating
it — that the confidential aside went into notes, not into anything client-facing.

## The morning brief (slide 9)

Answers when the skill asks:

> **Time:** 7:30
> **Ingredients:** Today's sessions · What you're owed · Growth calls · One suggested priority
> **Call target (growth calls):** five

Then the supervised Run now — approve everything, open the page, bookmark it on screen.

## The commitment (slide 12)

Yours, read first to model the shape:

> April will block a window for my five outreach calls every weekday morning.

And the message to the assistant:

> Hold me to this: five outreach calls every weekday. Put the window in my morning brief.

## Spare paste lines for open floor

> Who am I meeting this week that I need to prep for?

> What's fallen through the cracks?

> Research Copper Peak Builders' market — residential construction in northern Colorado —
> before their Quarterly.

> What do your notes say about Riverbend?

That last one doubles as the knowledge-graph demo: by open floor, the answer draws on the
interview, the taught fact, the recap, and the commitment — four inputs, one coherent client
picture, nothing retold twice.
